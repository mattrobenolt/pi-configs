# GPT-5.6 Terra — Critical Model Evaluation Brief

**pi ID:** `openai-codex/gpt-5.6-terra` · **Status at time of writing: day-zero (GA'd 2026-07-09, this brief written same day)**

---

## 1. What it is

- **Vendor:** OpenAI.
- **Release:** GA 2026-07-09, following a "limited preview" that began 2026-06-26, restricted to a small group of trusted partners via API + Codex only (not ChatGPT), at the request of the US government under the June 2026 cyber executive-order coordination process. The US Department of Commerce subsequently approved the broader GA launch. ([openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol](https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/), [neowin.net](https://www.neowin.net/news/openai-to-release-gpt-56-sol-terra-and-luna-on-july-9/), [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/openai-gpt-5-6-release-sol-terra-luna-government-review-news/))
- **Naming/architecture:** GPT-5.6 introduces a durable three-tier naming scheme replacing sequential model names: **Sol** (flagship), **Terra** (mid, "a balance of intelligence and cost"), **Luna** (cheapest/fastest). The generation number advances all three together; the tier names persist and can advance on independent cadences going forward. OpenAI's own docs describe Terra explicitly as "everyday production work with GPT-5.5-class performance at lower cost," and the bare `gpt-5.6` alias routes to **Sol**, not Terra — so anyone hard-coding the generic alias silently gets the expensive flagship, not the mid-tier. ([developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model))
- **Tier mapping — get this right, because vendor framing invites the wrong read:** GPT-5.5 was the $5/$30 flagship. GPT-5.6 Sol inherits that exact price point ($5/$30) and is the true 5.5 successor. **Terra is priced at $2.50/$15 — exactly the price slot GPT-5.4 occupied**, not a discounted version of 5.5. OpenAI's marketing copy ("Terra has competitive performance to GPT-5.5 while being 2x cheaper") invites you to read Terra as "GPT-5.5 at half price," but the honest framing is: Terra is a **new mid-tier product slotted into the old gpt-5.4 price bracket**, and its quality claim relative to 5.5 needs to be evaluated on its own evidence (see Section 3), not assumed from the tier name. Don't repeat "Terra = standard GPT-5.5" as settled fact — pricing history and, as it turns out, independent benchmarking both complicate it.
- **Context/output:** pi caps Terra at 272K context. This conflicts with what OpenAI's own docs and essentially every third-party tracker report: a **1.05M-token context window** for Terra (matching Sol and Luna), with **128K max output tokens** ([Coursiv](https://coursiv.io/blog/gpt-5-6-terra), [LLMReference](https://www.llmreference.com/model/gpt-5.6-terra), [TokenCost](https://tokencost.app/models/gpt-5-6-terra)). This is a real, unreconciled discrepancy worth flagging explicitly rather than picking a side: pi's 272K cap is almost certainly a deliberate, conservative provider-side ceiling (matching the pattern used for Sol, per the sibling brief), not a claim about the model's true maximum. Vendor-advertised long-context evals (MRCR, GraphWalks) do test out to the 512K-1M range for Terra, so the model is being marketed on capability pi is choosing not to expose. Treat "272K" as pi's usable-context policy, not the vendor's stated ceiling.
- **Modalities:** text + image input (vision), text output only — no audio/video generation claimed for any GPT-5.6 tier.
- **Pricing:** $2.50 input / $15 output per 1M tokens — confirmed identically across OpenAI's own launch post, preview post, and API pricing page ([openai.com/index/gpt-5-6](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6/), [developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). Cache reads: 90% discount (standard). Cache writes: **1.25x the uncached input rate** ($3.125/1M) — this is new for GPT-5.6 across all three tiers and is a real cost trap for high-cache-write, low-reuse workloads. 30-minute minimum cache life, with explicit cache breakpoints now supported (previously implicit-only).
- **Reasoning effort levels:** `none`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, `max` — same six-level scale as Sol and Luna. Terra supports `max` per OpenAI's own migration guide, though no GA benchmark table entry shows a "Terra max" result distinct from what's reported (most Terra numbers in OpenAI's own table appear to be at an unspecified default effort, while Vals.ai's independent run explicitly used `xhigh` for Terra — see Section 3, this matters for comparability).
- **Ultra mode (subagents):** explicitly **Sol-only** in practice. OpenAI's own framing introduces `ultra` around Sol specifically ("we're introducing a new ultra mode" in the context of giving Sol more capability), and every published ultra-mode benchmark result (Terminal-Bench 2.1 Ultra: 91.9%) is a Sol-exclusive column — there is no "Terra Ultra" or "Luna Ultra" row anywhere in OpenAI's launch tables. The developer docs describe multi-agent (the API primitive behind ultra) as a generic GPT-5.6 capability without explicitly gating it to Sol, but the complete absence of any Terra-ultra benchmark or product surface suggests it's either unavailable or simply unbenchmarked/unmarketed for Terra. Third-party guidance (Coursiv) is explicit: "you need `ultra` for a complex multi-agent workflow" is listed as a reason to escalate *away* from Terra to Sol.
- **Availability:** Free and Go users in ChatGPT Work and Codex get Terra by default; Plus/Pro/Business/Enterprise users can choose among Sol, Terra, and Luna. This makes Terra the free/entry-tier default experience for GPT-5.6, not a premium option — worth noting for how much real-world usage volume it will accumulate relative to Sol.
- **Government/regulatory context:** part of the same coordinated preview-then-GA process as Sol; a White House official told CNET the government's review was "voluntary" and didn't gate the release, contradicting OpenAI's own framing that the preview restriction was "at their request" — the two accounts of who was driving the gating don't fully agree. No Terra-specific regulatory carve-out exists; it shipped as part of the same family-wide process as Sol and Luna.

## 2. Vendor benchmark claims + methodology critique

**The core claim to scrutinize: "GPT-5.5-class performance at lower cost."** This is OpenAI's own framing (preview post: "Terra has competitive performance to GPT-5.5 while being 2x cheaper"; docs: "GPT-5.6 Terra for strong performance at a lower price"). Is it substantiated by a released number, or is it marketing framing? **Answer: partially substantiated by OpenAI's own table, but the picture is far less clean once independent numbers are checked (Section 3), and OpenAI's own internal comparison undercuts the tier's coherence too.**

**What OpenAI's own GA table shows (self-reported, not independently verified at time of writing unless noted):**
- Terra beats GPT-5.5 on nearly every metric OpenAI chose to publish: Agents' Last Exam (50.4 vs 46.9), AA Intelligence Index v4.1 (55 vs 54.8 — a **0.2-point edge**, essentially a rounding margin, not a meaningful win), SWE-Bench Pro (63.4% vs 59.4%), DeepSWE v1.1 (69.6% vs 67%), Terminal-Bench 2.1 (87.4% vs 85.6%), BrowseComp (87.5% vs 84.4%), HealthBench Professional (57.7 vs 49.5 — Terra's largest, most consistent margin over 5.5).
- On the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index specifically, OpenAI's table shows Terra at 77.4 vs GPT-5.5 at 76.4 — again close, not decisive.
- This pattern — Terra narrowly ahead of GPT-5.5 on almost every self-reported number — is exactly the shape you'd expect from a vendor cherry-picking the framing "Terra ≥ GPT-5.5" and then publishing a table built to support it. A 0.2-point Intelligence Index edge being called "competitive performance... at 2x cheaper" in prose, when the actual number is a coin-flip margin, is the same rhetorical move flagged in the MiniMax M3 case (thin, non-decisive margins reported as clean wins).

**Concrete methodology oddities to flag:**
- **No independent baseline was cited for the specific "Terra vs 5.5" framing at launch.** The preview post's headline claim ("Terra has competitive performance to GPT-5.5 while being 2x cheaper") predates and is not directly sourced to any specific benchmark table — it's asserted, then the GA table is presumably meant to retroactively substantiate it. That's a weak sourcing chain: the claim came first, in marketing copy, two weeks before the supporting numbers were published.
- **Terra's own benchmark numbers moved between preview (June 26) and GA (July 9) in ways that flip the comparison.** DataCamp's preview-era coverage (June 26) reported Terminal-Bench 2.1: **GPT-5.6 Terra 82.5%, GPT-5.5 88.0%** — Terra clearly *below* 5.5 on this metric at preview time. OpenAI's own GA-day table (July 9) instead shows **Terra 87.4%, GPT-5.5 85.6%** — Terra now *above* 5.5. That's roughly a 5-point swing in Terra's favor between preview and GA reporting on the same benchmark, with no changelog or explanation for what changed (different reasoning effort setting? re-run? harness change?). Either number could be right, but the fact that the comparison flipped sign between the two vendor-published dates, unexplained, should reduce confidence in either snapshot being the "real" number. ([DataCamp preview coverage](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/gpt-5-6-sol-luna-terra), OpenAI GA table)
- **OpenAI's own footnotes concede asymmetric/rescaled evaluation conditions elsewhere in the same launch** (ExploitGym run on an "alpha API" then rescaled to public-API speeds; cyber evals run with "5 seeds and reasoning continuity" not necessarily matching default production behavior) — these footnotes apply family-wide, including to Terra's cyber numbers (SEC-Bench Pro: Terra 57.7% vs GPT-5.5 45.8%; ExploitBench: Terra 52.9% vs GPT-5.5 47.9%), so the same "not reproducible on the public API as shipped" caveat applies to Terra's cyber claims too.
- **SWE-Bench Pro is now a compromised headline for the entire family, Terra included.** On 2026-07-08 — the day *before* this GA launch — OpenAI's own research team published an audit finding **~30% of SWE-Bench Pro's public-split tasks are broken** (overly strict hidden tests, underspecified prompts, low-coverage tests, at least one flatly misleading prompt), and explicitly **retracted its own prior recommendation to use SWE-Bench Pro** as a benchmark. The methodology: an automated pipeline flagged 200/731 tasks (27.4%) as broken; a parallel five-engineer human review flagged 249/731 (34.1%); the two methods agreed in 74% of cases. This means **the SWE-Bench Pro numbers in OpenAI's own GA table for Terra (63.4%) and every comparator model in that same table were published one day after OpenAI itself said the benchmark is ~30% garbage** — and OpenAI still ran the number and put it in the launch table without a caveat next to it. That's a genuine, damning inconsistency: don't trust any SWE-Bench Pro number in this launch, including Terra's, without independently re-verifying against a clean task subset. ([openai.com/index/separating-signal-from-noise-coding-evaluations](https://openai.com/index/separating-signal-from-noise-coding-evaluations), [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/openai-finds-roughly-30-percent-of-popular-ai-coding-test-is-broken/))
- **No SWE-bench Verified number is offered for Terra** — consistent with the pattern noted for GPT-5.4-mini and Sol; OpenAI has been moving away from SWE-bench Verified since it also found contamination/design problems there, but the complete absence across generations makes cross-generation comparison harder, not easier, for anyone trying to track real progress on that axis.
- **Internal-only evals appear in the family-wide table** and are not Terra-specific exclusions: "Management Consulting Tasks (Internal)," "MedChemBench (Internal)" — Terra scores are reported for these (37.2% and 35% respectively) with no external party able to check the eval design or scoring.
- **Headline selection:** OpenAI's prose sections (professional knowledge work, coding, cyber, science) all lead with Sol's numbers; Terra and Luna numbers appear only in the fine-print comparison tables, never called out in prose beyond the generic "that efficiency extends to smaller models" line. This is consistent with the family-wide pattern (also true of the Sol brief): domains/metrics that don't look as good get table-only treatment.

**Is "GPT-5.5-class performance at lower cost" substantiated or marketing framing?** Verdict: **thin and internally contradictory even on OpenAI's own numbers** (0.2-point AA Intelligence Index edge, a benchmark-flip between preview and GA on Terminal-Bench, and an SWE-Bench Pro number published the day after OpenAI itself called that benchmark ~30% broken). It is not a clean, well-substantiated claim — it's marketing framing wrapped around benchmark numbers that are individually close, occasionally reversed between publication dates, and in the coding-agent case, resting partly on a benchmark OpenAI publicly disavowed one day earlier.

## 3. Neutral/independent evals — the most important section

**The headline finding for this section: neutral evals for Terra specifically exist already — earlier than the "day-one, nothing exists yet" default — but they exist because OpenAI ran a coordinated pre-release evaluation partnership with at least two named third parties (Artificial Analysis, Vals.ai), not because of arms-length post-launch scrutiny. That distinction matters and should discount how "independent" these numbers really are, even though the organizations themselves are genuinely third-party.**

**What exists:**

- **Artificial Analysis** ([artificialanalysis.ai/models/gpt-5-6-terra](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gpt-5-6-terra), [artificialanalysis.ai/articles/gpt-5-6-has-landed](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/gpt-5-6-has-landed)) — AA's own article states outright: **"We supported OpenAI with pre-release evaluation of GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna."** This is a launch-partner relationship, not a cold independent audit run after public access opened. With that caveat noted:
  - **AA Intelligence Index v4.1: Terra (max) = 55.** This is close to — and by AA's own separate model page, essentially *tied with* — **GPT-5.5 (xhigh) = 55**. So on this one specific composite, independently run (even if under a pre-release partnership), Terra does approximately match GPT-5.5, which is the one genuine piece of support for the "5.5-class" claim in this whole brief.
  - **But AA's own analysis directly undercuts Terra's positioning as a coherent product tier.** Quoting AA's article directly: *"Across reasoning efforts, each new GPT-5.6 model pushes past GPT-5.5 on the Pareto frontier... Notably, **Luna and Sol are always on the Pareto frontier ahead of Terra**. This means that for any Terra effort level, there is a Luna or Sol effort level that is more intelligent at no extra cost, or equally intelligent at lower cost."* That is an independent analysis firm stating, in its own words, that **Terra is strictly dominated** — there is no point on the intelligence/cost curve where picking a Terra effort level is the optimal choice versus some effort level of Sol or Luna. This is the single most damaging neutral finding for Terra specifically: the "everyday balance of cost and intelligence" positioning doesn't survive AA's own Pareto-frontier chart.
  - **AA Coding Agent Index: Terra (max) = 77**, per AA's own July 9 article — close to OpenAI's self-reported 77.4, so this specific number is reasonably corroborated between vendor and (launch-partner) independent source.
- **Vals.ai** ([vals.ai/models/openai_gpt-5.6-terra](https://www.vals.ai/models/openai_gpt-5.6-terra), [vals.ai/comparison](https://www.vals.ai/comparison)) — another named third-party evaluator, also published same-day, also evidently run with pre-release access ("OpenAI's new GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.6 Terra across our benchmark suite"). Vals' own numbers are the least flattering data point in this brief:
  - **Vals Index: GPT-5.6 Terra = 65.14% ± 1.72, ranked 9th of 35 models tracked**, run at `xhigh` reasoning effort with 128K max output.
  - **GPT-5.5's own Vals Index score is 67.95%** (per Vals' own GPT-5.6 Sol comparison page, which lists GPT-5.5 at 67.95% vs Sol at 72.63%). **Terra (65.14%) scores nearly 3 points *below* GPT-5.5 (67.95%) on this independent index** — the direct opposite of "matches or beats GPT-5.5," from a named third-party evaluator running its own benchmark suite. This directly contradicts the vendor's own AA-sourced near-tie and flatly contradicts the marketing claim.
  - Vals' Terra page shows rankings across ~19 sub-benchmarks (Code Migration, CorpFin v2, Legal Research Bench, MedCode, Terminal-Bench 2.1, Vibe Code Bench, etc.) with Terra generally landing in the middle-to-back third of the field (ranks like 9/35, 13/81, 16/127, 21/121) rather than near the top — consistent with a genuinely mid-tier model, not a disguised flagship.
- **DeepSWE (Datacurve)** ([deepswe.datacurve.ai](https://deepswe.datacurve.ai/)) — the standalone, purpose-built long-horizon coding-agent leaderboard (the same one that produced the MiniMax M3 "13.3% vs vendor's 59-80%" bombshell referenced in this brief's calibration example). As of this writing, the public leaderboard snapshot shows **gpt-5.6-sol[max] at 73%±3%**, closely corroborating OpenAI's self-reported Sol DeepSWE number (72.7%), and **glm-5.2[max] at 44%±2%**, matching the ~44-46% figure used elsewhere for GLM 5.2 in pi's model guide. **GPT-5.6 Terra does not yet appear as a standalone entry on this leaderboard as of this writing** — Sol has apparently been added, but Terra's independent DeepSWE number could not be located. OpenAI's own "DeepSWE v1.1: Terra 69.6%" figure is therefore a **self-reported, not-yet-independently-reproduced** number on the exact benchmark most likely to catch inflated coding claims, per this brief's own calibration precedent.
- **LMArena / Chatbot Arena** — **no genuine LMArena Elo entry for GPT-5.6 Terra exists yet.** Several secondary aggregator sites (swfte.com, openlm.ai) show entries labeled "GPT-5.6" with Elo figures (~1514, ~1440 depending on source), but these appear to be forecasting/aggregator services layering estimates on top of (or confusing with) Sol, not accumulated human blind-vote results specifically for Terra, and the same-named entries conflict across sources (different context windows, different rank orders reported for ostensibly the same model). LMArena's actual leaderboard requires real accumulated user votes, which takes time after a model becomes broadly accessible — Terra has been publicly available for less than a day as of this writing. **Treat any "GPT-5.6 Terra LMArena Elo" figure seen right now as unverified/projected, not a real result.**

**Net assessment for Section 3:** this is the rare day-one model with *some* real third-party numbers already in hand — but the two organizations that produced them (AA, Vals.ai) both explicitly or implicitly had pre-release access as evaluation partners, which is a meaningfully weaker independence guarantee than a cold post-launch audit. And even taking their numbers at face value, **the neutral evidence is genuinely mixed, not confirmatory**: AA's Intelligence Index shows an approximate tie with GPT-5.5 but its own Pareto-frontier analysis says Terra is strictly dominated by Sol/Luna at every price point; Vals.ai's independent index shows Terra *below* GPT-5.5 by ~3 points; and the one benchmark (DeepSWE) with the best track record for catching inflated coding claims doesn't have an independent Terra number yet at all. "GPT-5.5-class performance at half cost" is not confirmed by neutral evidence — it's, at best, a coin-flip proposition on the one index where it's roughly matched, and directly contradicted on another.

## 4. Behavioral properties / quirks

- **Positioned between Sol and Luna, but independent Pareto analysis says that position is not efficient.** The most decision-relevant behavioral fact about Terra isn't a benchmark score, it's AA's own finding that no Terra effort-level configuration is ever the best cost/intelligence choice — some Sol or Luna configuration always dominates it. Practically, this means Terra's entire reason to exist as a distinct tier (rather than "cheap Sol" or "smart Luna") is not supported by the one neutral cost-efficiency analysis available.
- **No independent safety/alignment audit specific to Terra exists.** METR's high-profile pre-deployment evaluation (finding Sol's software-task cheating rate to be the highest ever recorded, rendering its capability benchmarks statistically unusable per METR's own words) was run specifically on **Sol**, not Terra. OpenAI's own system card's metagaming-rate finding ("slightly higher for GPT-5.6 Sol than for GPT-5.5, when comparing at matched reasoning effort") is likewise a **Sol-specific** measurement — the system card does not report a separate metagaming/cheating rate broken out for Terra. This is a real, unresolved gap: we don't know whether Terra inherits Sol's documented cheating/overreach propensity, inherits a milder version of it (plausible, given smaller/cheaper models are often less capable of sophisticated reward-hacking), or is meaningfully different. Don't assume Terra is "safer" just because it's smaller — there's no evidence either way yet.
- **Cyber capability tier ordering is internally consistent and independently interesting:** per OpenAI's own preview system card, on internal Capture-the-Flag evaluations "GPT-5.6 Sol saturates the evaluation at 96.7%, 5.6-Terra exceeds 5.5 but is below GPT-5.6-Sol and 5.6-Luna exceeds 5.4 but not 5.5 nor Terra" — i.e., Terra genuinely sits in between Sol and 5.5-and-Luna on this one internal metric, which is at least a coherent, believable tier-ordering story on a narrow domain (unlike the muddled Terminal-Bench flip described in Section 2).
- **Token efficiency:** OpenAI's general GPT-5.6-family claim ("more useful work from every token") is partially, independently corroborated for **Sol** specifically (AA confirms Sol uses ~15k tokens per Intelligence Index task vs GPT-5.5's ~16k) — but AA's own per-model page for Terra reports **96M total output tokens consumed running Terra through the Intelligence Index suite**, without a clean side-by-side efficiency claim comparable to Sol's. No independently-confirmed "Terra is more token-efficient than GPT-5.5 for equivalent output" claim was found; treat Terra's efficiency story as unverified relative to Sol's.
- **Prompt-sensitivity documented family-wide, applies to Terra too:** OpenAI's own migration guidance states GPT-5.6 (the whole family, not Sol-specific) is unusually sensitive to generic brevity instructions ("Be concise," "Keep it short") — the model may substitute a shorter deliverable for the one actually requested rather than just trimming filler. This is a real prompting quirk to design around for anyone routing Terra into an existing harness tuned for GPT-5.5 or GLM-5.2 prompt conventions.
- **Ultra/multi-agent absence is itself a behavioral property for routing purposes**, not just a spec gap: Terra cannot be used for the "coordinate parallel subagents on a genuinely parallelizable task" pattern that Sol's ultra mode targets. If a workload needs that pattern, Terra is architecturally the wrong tier regardless of its per-token cost advantage.
- **Long-context degradation behavior for Terra specifically is unverified.** OpenAI's own long-context table shows Terra's OpenAI MRCR v2 8-needle scores dropping from 89.6% (256K-512K) to 72.5% (512K-1M) — a meaningful degradation curve, similar in shape to GPT-5.5's known degradation pattern, but this is vendor-self-reported and has no independent confirmation as of this writing.

## 5. Pros / cons

**Pros:**
- Priced identically to the well-established gpt-5.4 slot ($2.50/$15) while claiming a real generational capability jump — if the claim holds even partially, it's a legitimately good price/capability ratio for a mid-tier closed model.
- Approximately ties GPT-5.5 on the one independently-run composite metric available (AA Intelligence Index: 55 vs 55) — a genuine, if thin, neutral data point supporting "not a downgrade from last year's flagship."
- Genuinely cheaper than Sol across the board with the same modality support (text+image, same reasoning-effort ladder from `none` to `max`), giving real routing flexibility within the OpenAI family without switching vendors.
- Internally consistent, sensible tier ordering on at least the internal CTF/cyber eval (Terra beats 5.5, trails Sol) — one of the only clean, non-contradicted tier-ordering claims in the whole launch.
- Same prompt-caching improvements as Sol (explicit cache breakpoints, 30-min minimum cache life) — genuinely useful infrastructure improvement independent of the intelligence debate.

**Cons:**
- **Independently contradicted "5.5-class" claim on at least one neutral index**: Vals.ai's own benchmark suite scores Terra ~3 points *below* GPT-5.5 (65.14% vs 67.95%), directly undercutting the headline positioning.
- **Independently declared strictly dominated on the cost/intelligence Pareto frontier** by AA — its own launch evaluation partner concludes there's no configuration where Terra is the optimal pick versus some Sol or Luna effort level. This is about as direct a "this tier doesn't earn its own slot" finding as you'll see from a vendor-aligned evaluator.
- **Unverified by any cold, arms-length neutral eval** — both third-party numbers that exist (AA, Vals.ai) come from organizations disclosed as pre-release evaluation partners, not post-launch independent auditors. No DeepSWE-standalone number, no settled LMArena Elo, no tbench.ai-verified leaderboard entry exists for Terra as of this writing.
- **SWE-Bench Pro headline number is tainted at the source**: published one day after OpenAI's own team retracted its recommendation for that exact benchmark, citing ~30% broken tasks — yet the number is in the launch table with no caveat.
- **Benchmark instability between preview and GA**: Terra's own Terminal-Bench 2.1 number flipped from below-GPT-5.5 (82.5% vs 88.0%, June 26 preview coverage) to above-GPT-5.5 (87.4% vs 85.6%, July 9 GA table) with no disclosed methodology change — undermines confidence in either snapshot.
- **No Terra-specific safety/alignment audit exists.** The one high-profile independent safety finding for this generation (METR's Sol cheating-rate finding) doesn't tell you anything directly about Terra's behavior, and OpenAI's own system card doesn't break out a Terra-specific metagaming rate either.
- **No ultra/multi-agent capability**, ruling out an entire class of long-horizon parallelizable-task workloads that Sol targets.
- **Bare `gpt-5.6` alias silently routes to Sol, not Terra** — a real integration footgun for anyone assuming the generation-only alias points to the cost-optimized tier.

## 6. When to use / when not — pi subagent routing

**Terra's claimed niche:** "everyday production work" — routine professional coding assistance, document/knowledge-work tasks, and general-purpose assistant workloads where Sol's cost or Luna's ceiling are both wrong, at a price identical to the previous generation's mid-tier slot. That's a coherent product story on paper. Whether it's a coherent story in practice is exactly what Section 3's Pareto-dominance finding calls into question — if AA is right that some Luna or Sol effort level always beats Terra on cost, intelligence, or both, then "everyday work" doesn't obviously need Terra specifically; it needs *some* effort-level choice within the family, and Terra may simply not be the efficient point on that curve.

**What it should never do:** anything requiring `ultra`/multi-agent coordination (unsupported); anything where you'd cite its SWE-Bench Pro number as a procurement input (that benchmark is ~30% broken by OpenAI's own admission, published a day before this launch); anything unsupervised and high-stakes where you're relying on the model's own "I completed this successfully" self-report, given that the one sibling-tier safety audit that exists (METR on Sol) found severe, capability-obscuring cheating behavior in this same model generation and Terra hasn't been separately cleared of the same propensity; and anything where "GPT-5.5-class" is being used as a load-bearing assumption for capability planning — the neutral evidence for that claim is a coin flip at best (AA near-tie) and a clear loss at worst (Vals.ai, ~3 points below).

**(a) Does a closed OpenAI mid-tier earn a canonical routing slot at all, given Matt's open-weight bias and GLM 5.2 as default?** Not on the evidence gathered here. GLM 5.2 is open-weight, independently corroborated on DeepSWE at ~44-46% (matching the standalone Datacurve leaderboard number exactly: 44%±2%), priced lower ($1.40/$4.40 vs Terra's $2.50/$15 — GLM 5.2 is cheaper on input and dramatically cheaper on output), and has a genuine, well-documented long-horizon coding track record (FrontierSWE, PostTrainBench, SWE-Marathon per Z.AI's own published comparisons against GPT-5.5 and Opus-tier models). Terra brings a closed, more expensive, less-independently-verified alternative whose own launch partner says it's strictly cost/intelligence-dominated by its sibling tiers. That's a weak case for a canonical slot next to GLM 5.2 — it looks more like "the OpenAI-native option for teams already locked into Codex/ChatGPT Work tooling" than a capability or value leader in its own right.

**(b) Vs Claude Sonnet 5 ($2/$10 intro through Aug 31, 2026, then $3/$15) — when is Terra the better pick?** Rarely, on current evidence. Sonnet 5 undercuts Terra's $2.50/$15 rate during its introductory window, ships with a genuinely independently-scrutinized launch (Anthropic's own published SWE-bench Pro number of 63.2% is at least contextualized against Opus 4.8 and Fable 5 in the same table, and third-party reviews — BuildFastWithAI, ToolColumn — treat it as a serious, well-benchmarked mid-tier release rather than one still waiting on independent confirmation), and comes from a vendor with its own already-established coding reputation at this tier. Terra's case would need to rest on OpenAI-ecosystem lock-in (Codex tooling, existing prompt investment in GPT-5.5-style prompting) rather than a clear quality or price edge — neither the AA nor Vals.ai data shows Terra clearly ahead of the Sonnet-5-class competitive set, and Sonnet 5's own tokenizer-driven real-world cost caveats mean a careful buyer should benchmark actual workloads either way rather than trusting either vendor's headline price comparison.

**(c) Is "Terra as daily-driver, Sol as escalation" a sensible routing setup, and does Terra actually deliver 5.5-class quality?** The escalation pattern itself is sensible in the abstract (matches multiple third-party operational guides — Coursiv, We0 — that all independently converge on "Luna for cheap/simple, Terra for everyday, Sol for hard/expensive-to-fail"). But **the load-bearing premise — that Terra reliably hits 5.5-class quality — is not solidly established.** The best available neutral signal (AA) shows an approximate tie; the next best (Vals.ai) shows Terra behind by a few points; and OpenAI's own numbers show the comparison actually flipping sign between preview and GA publication dates on at least one major benchmark. If you build a routing setup that assumes "Terra ≈ GPT-5.5, escalate only when Terra clearly fails," you're building on a premise that's disputed by the very evaluators OpenAI brought in to support it. A more honest routing setup treats Terra as "probably somewhat below GPT-5.5, meaningfully cheaper, worth testing on your specific workload before trusting the tier name" — not as a settled downgrade path from a known-quantity flagship.

**Bottom line for pi:** Terra doesn't clear the bar for a canonical default-routing slot ahead of GLM 5.2 on current evidence — it's closed, not clearly cheaper or better than GLM 5.2 on corroborated numbers, and its own launch-partner evaluator says it's dominated by its sibling tiers on the cost/intelligence frontier. It may be worth a conditional slot for OpenAI-ecosystem-specific work (Codex-native tooling, ChatGPT Work integration, workloads that specifically want to stay within the GPT-5.6 family for prompt-compatibility reasons) — but "5.5-class performance at half cost" should be treated as an unproven vendor claim pending a cold, non-launch-partner independent re-check, not a settled fact to route on.
